Zero Trust Architecture (NIST SP 800-207), as conventionally built, is policy-driven and depends on a reachable central authority (CA/OCSP/CRL) on the connection hot path. That premise fails at the tactical edge: it does not survive comms-denied (DDIL) conditions, where large CRLs cannot cross 9.6 to 256 kbps SATCOM and ZTA collapses to fail-open or fail-closed; it cannot see behavior, so a compromised but credentialed endpoint passes every check; and it does not scale by policy.

AutonomousTrust (AT) is a working prototype that extends ZTA with continuous, autonomous, behavioral trust evaluation running without centralized infrastructure. Authentication opens the door; AT enforcement then governs what a peer may do and revokes it in real time. AT deploys as a resident agent or a bump-in-the-wire gateway acting as each node's policy decision and enforcement point, with a bounded-memory C core and a streaming anomaly layer sized to a tight node budget.

AT layers five bases of trust: authentication/provenance (ICAM), hardware attestation, least-privilege containment, delegated trust, and behavioral reputation. Bases 1 to 4 need no history and carry first contact; behavioral ML refines trust over the fleet's lifetime as a governed sensor rather than the foundation. Machine identity is cryptographic (Ed25519, X25519, UUID; keys never leave the node). A purpose-built permissioned DLT provides a Merkle-DAG identity ledger, a Merkle-checkpointed reputation ledger, and an append-only audit log, avoiding a global chain and Proof-of-Work.

Trust changes are agreed via leaderless Byzantine Multi-Paxos. A reputation slash is quorum-signed and Merkle-proof-gated, then triggers autonomous tier-gate exclusion; every decision is deterministic and traceable to signed evidence. Micro-segmentation enforces two orthogonal axes, a hierarchical criticality tier and a need-to-know compartment set, using lattice/dominance semantics. Under jamming or partition, a sub-threshold quorum admits only at a capped reputation ceiling and reduced tier, gated by history-independent identity and attestation; every admission is signed and reconciles on reconnect, so the system never waits on a reachable CA.

The production C core is under Frama-C/WP + ACSL formal verification (100% on attempted goals, ~54% function coverage) as an ATO asset, with a byte-for-byte Python reference pinned by a conformance corpus. Phase I substantiates four targets by M&S and subscale silicon: authentication time under 5 s (measured), latency reduced 50%, unauthorized access reduced 90%, and admin overhead reduced 25%, against a pre-registered ZTA baseline and an ATT&CK-mapped attack corpus delivered as reproducible Government artifacts.
